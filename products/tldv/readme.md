# tl;dv

> Meeting recordings turned into timestamped clips and insights.

## Editorial review

### Verdict

tl;dv is well suited to teams that need to turn conversations into shareable evidence. Timestamped notes and short clips help product, sales, and research teams show exactly what a customer said without asking colleagues to watch a full recording. Its broader analysis features are most useful when there is enough meeting volume to reveal patterns. Smaller teams may only need the core recording and summary workflow.

### Recommended workflow

Agree on a small set of tags for themes such as objections, feature requests, competitors, and decisions. Highlight relevant moments during or immediately after each call, then share focused clips in the place where decisions are made. Use multi-meeting analysis only after transcript quality and tagging are consistent. Review any automated claim before it enters a roadmap, sales forecast, or research report.

### Value assessment

The free plan provides a practical way to test recording, summaries, and sharing. Paid plans are easier to justify when CRM automation, team controls, or cross-meeting insights replace manual research synthesis. Value depends on reuse: a clip that informs a product decision or sales handoff is more useful than a large passive recording library. Include storage and governance in the purchasing decision.

### Alternatives to consider

Fathom offers a streamlined individual experience. Fireflies.ai provides broad meeting search and integration options. Otter focuses more on the live transcript. Dedicated user-research repositories may be a better fit when teams need participant management, consent workflows, coding, and research governance in addition to call capture.
