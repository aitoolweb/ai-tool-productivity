## Editorial review

### Verdict

Grammarly remains useful because it improves writing where the work already happens. Instead of moving every email or document into a chatbot, users receive focused suggestions for correctness, clarity, and tone. That low-friction approach is valuable for high-volume professional communication. The main limitation is that accepting too many rewrites can flatten a distinctive voice or introduce wording that is technically polished but less precise.

### Recommended workflow

Install Grammarly only in the browsers and applications where assistance is needed, then adjust goals for audience, formality, and tone. Use correctness suggestions continuously, but treat larger rewrites as alternatives rather than automatic fixes. For important documents, complete the argument and factual review first, then use Grammarly as the final editing pass. A personal style guide helps decide when the product's preference should be ignored.

### Value assessment

The free plan covers enough grammar and clarity support for many users. A paid plan is more valuable for people who write externally facing material every day and regularly use tone or rewrite features. The decision should depend on editing volume, not on the novelty of generative features. Teams should compare centralized style controls and administration with the cost of providing separate accounts.

### Alternatives to consider

LanguageTool and built-in office-suite editors can cover basic correction at lower cost. ChatGPT and Claude are better for structural rewriting, ideation, and explaining why a passage is unclear. Jasper offers stronger marketing workflow and brand controls. Choose Grammarly when continuous in-context editing matters more than generating a first draft.
